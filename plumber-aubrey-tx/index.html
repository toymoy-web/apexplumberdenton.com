New house, new plumbing, and still a problem?

People expect a new home's plumbing to be trouble-free. Usually it is, but the first few years bring their own issues: a fitting that was not fully crimped, a drain line knocked out of place during construction, a slab leak as fresh clay settles under the foundation.

New homes in North Texas are built and inspected to the International Plumbing Code (IPC) family of codes that cities adopt with local amendments. An inspection catches a lot, but it cannot test every fitting under real household use for years.

If your home is still under the builder's warranty, report plumbing problems to the builder in writing first. If the problem is urgent, stop the water, document it and get it fixed, then share the plumber's report with the builder.

Outside warranty, the common calls in newer Aubrey homes are water heater and fixture issues, running toilets, and hidden leaks found through a high bill. Leak detection is the place to start when the bill does not add up.

Who supplies your water in Aubrey matters

Aubrey has split water service. Some neighborhoods, such as Venable Ranch, are served by the City of Aubrey. Much of the surrounding area is served by Mustang Special Utility District, headquartered on FM 2931 in Aubrey.

Mustang's reports show a mixed supply: groundwater from the Trinity and Woodbine aquifers, surface water bought from the Upper Trinity Regional Water District from Jim Chapman and Lewisville lakes, and groundwater purchased from the City of Sherman.

Aubrey's 76227 ZIP also takes in Providence Village, Cross Roads and Krugerville, so neighbors in those towns use the same line and often the same utility providers.

A mixed, well-fed supply can carry sediment when something upstream fails. WFAA reported that residents of the Silverado subdivision saw brown, then cloudy water after a well's bottom screen failed and let sand into the system. The utility said the water was not unsafe. For your plumbing, the practical lesson is that sand ends up in aerators, cartridges and water heater tanks.

The same sediment and water heater questions come up across the ten-mile radius around Aubrey, including Pilot Point in 76258 and east Denton in 76208.

What to do after a sediment or cloudy-water event

These steps protect fixtures and appliances once your provider says the water is back to normal.

  • Run cold water at the tub spout or an outdoor faucet until it clears.
  • Unscrew and rinse faucet aerators and showerhead screens.
  • Flush the water heater to clear sand from the bottom of the tank.
  • Check the inlet screen on a tankless heater, washing machine and ice maker.
  • If sediment keeps showing up, consider a whole-house sediment filter at the main line.

Tank flushing and heater checks are part of water heater repair, and filter options are on the water filtration page.
