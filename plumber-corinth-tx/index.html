What Corinth homeowners call about most

Corinth's housing is newer than central Denton's, so the call mix is different. These come up most often.

  • Slab leaks. Supply lines run under the foundation in most Corinth homes, and the clay underneath moves with every wet and dry season.
  • Garage water heaters reaching the end of their life. Many homes built in the 1990s and 2000s are on their second or third heater, and some original installs lack drain pans or proper relief-valve lines.
  • High water bills with no visible leak. Usually a toilet flapper, an irrigation zone or a line under the slab.
  • Kitchen drain clogs in homes where the original lines have never been cleaned.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak in Corinth

These four steps limit the damage while a plumber is on the way.

Close the main

In most Corinth slab homes the house shutoff sits in the garage wall or near the water heater. The city meter box by the curb has a second valve if the house valve sticks.

Cut power to wet areas

If water is reaching a garage outlet, the laundry room or a floor-level plug, switch that circuit off at the breaker. Only do it from a dry spot.

Clear the immediate area

Pull rugs, boxes and anything stored on the floor away from the leak. On tile over a slab, water runs under baseboards fast, so check the next room too.

Why Corinth slab homes develop leaks

The soil under Corinth is the same expansive clay found across southern Denton County. It swells after heavy rain and shrinks through dry summers, and a slab foundation rises and settles with it.

Water lines cast into or under that slab cannot flex much. After years of movement, a copper line can wear a pinhole where it rubs concrete, or a fitting can crack. The first signs are small: a warm spot on tile, a hissing sound in a hallway, or a meter that never stops.

The fix depends on the pipe and the house. A single leak in otherwise sound pipe is often repaired through a small opening in the floor. Repeated leaks usually mean rerouting the line overhead. Our slab leak repair page walks through both options.

Corinth's water supply and your fixtures

The City of Corinth buys treated surface water from the Upper Trinity Regional Water District, which draws it from Lewisville Lake. The city's annual water quality reports show it meeting federal standards, and Corinth received the TCEQ's Outstanding Public Drinking Water Safety Award in 2019.

Safe water can still leave mineral film behind. Scale on showerheads and sediment in the bottom of a water heater are common in Corinth homes, just as they are in Denton. A yearly tank flush is the cheapest protection, and our page on fixing a rumbling or leaking tank covers the next step.

If your household wants softer water, test your own tap first. Lake water varies by season, and the reading at your faucet is what matters for sizing a softener.
