Does Denton Require a Permit to Replace a Water Heater?

By the Denton Apex Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026.

Replacing a water heater feels like swapping an appliance. In code terms, it is plumbing, gas or electrical, and venting work. That is why most cities, Denton included, expect a water heater permit and an inspection. This guide explains what that involves and why it is worth doing.

The short answer

Yes. The City of Denton's own Water Heater permit page says that replacing, adding or changing the size of a water heater requires a plumbing permit. Requirements and fees can change, so confirm the current rules with the city's Development Services department before work starts.

In practice, a licensed plumber pulls the permit as part of the job and schedules the inspection. The homeowner mainly needs to make the heater accessible on inspection day.

Denton adopted the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), effective June 2022, with regional amendments. Inspectors check the new installation against that code and its companion fuel gas and electrical codes.

Why the permit matters

Water heaters combine pressurized hot water, fuel or high-amperage power, and exhaust. Most of the serious hazards come from installation mistakes: a relief valve drain that is capped or run the wrong way, a vent that slopes downward or leaks exhaust, or a gas connection without a proper shutoff.

An inspection is a second set of eyes on exactly those items. It is also a record. When you sell a house, buyers and their inspectors may ask about the water heater's installation. When you file an insurance claim, an unpermitted install can raise questions.

Skipping the permit rarely saves much, and it shifts all of that risk onto the homeowner.

What an inspector typically checks

Exact items vary by installation. These come up on most water heater inspections.

How the process usually goes

For a standard same-type replacement, it looks like this.

  • The plumber confirms the new unit's size and type and checks the existing gas line, venting and electrical supply.
  • The plumber applies for the permit with the City of Denton.
  • The old heater is removed and the new one installed, with any code corrections made along the way.
  • The water heater is put into service so the household has hot water.
  • An inspector visits to check the installation, usually within days. Someone may need to provide access.
  • If anything needs correcting, the plumber fixes it and the inspection is completed.

Common code corrections on older Denton installs

Replacing a heater that went in 15 or 20 years ago often turns up a few items that no longer meet code. These are the usual ones.

  • A relief valve line that is missing, undersized or capped.
  • No drain pan under a heater in an attic or interior closet.
  • An old flexible gas connector that should be replaced with the new unit.
  • Vent connectors with poor slope or loose joints.
  • No expansion tank where the water system is closed by a check valve or pressure reducing valve.

These corrections add some cost, but they are the parts of the job that most affect safety.

What to do if something seems wrong after an install

A new water heater should run quietly and safely. These are signs to stop and call right away.

  • A gas smell near the heater. Leave the house and call Atmos Energy or 911 from outside.
  • Soot or scorch marks around the burner opening or vent hood, which point to a venting or combustion problem.
  • A carbon monoxide alarm going off. Get everyone outside and call 911.
  • Water dripping from the relief valve pipe repeatedly, which can mean pressure or temperature is too high.
  • Water at the base or fittings in the first days after installation.
  • No hot water, or water far hotter than the setting, which points to a control problem.

A permitted installation has a paper trail and an installer who is accountable for fixing these issues. That is one more reason to use the permit process.

Emergencies and permits

When a tank fails and water is on the floor, nobody wants to wait for paperwork. In most emergency replacements the plumber installs the new heater right away and completes the permit process promptly afterward, as local rules allow. Ask your plumber how they handle it.

For a planned replacement, there is time to choose the right unit. Our water heater installation page covers sizing and types, and if the swap involves a new or resized gas line, the gas line installation and repair page explains that side.

What about nearby towns?

Each city sets its own permit rules and adopts its own code editions and amendments. Most North Texas cities follow a similar approach, but fees, forms and inspection scheduling differ.

If you live in a nearby town such as Sanger, your plumber pulls the permit from that city rather than Denton. Homes outside city limits may fall under county or no local permitting, though good installation practice still applies.

Denton note: what the city's permit page lists

The City of Denton's Water Heater page says replacing, adding or changing the size of a water heater requires a plumbing permit. The application is the MEP Application, submitted online through eTRAKiT, and the listed inspection is a plumbing final.

Adding a heater or changing its size also requires plumbing plans and a floor plan. The page asks you to state the scope, such as a like-for-like or different-size replacement. Argyle and other towns set their own permit rules.

Some cities allow homeowners to permit work on their own homes, with conditions. Ask the City of Denton's Development Services department what applies before doing the work yourself.
