Heat Pump vs Gas Water Heater in Denton

By the Denton Apex Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026.

When a water heater fails, most people replace it with whatever was there before. That is often the right call, but it is not the only one. Heat pump water heaters have become a real option for many North Texas homes. This guide compares them with gas tanks, the most common choice in Denton garages.

How each one heats water

A gas water heater burns natural gas under the tank, and the exhaust leaves through a vent. In Denton, gas service comes from Atmos Energy. Gas tanks heat water quickly and keep working during a power outage if they have a standing pilot, though many newer models need electricity for ignition or controls.

A heat pump water heater works like an air conditioner in reverse. It pulls heat from the surrounding air and moves it into the water. Because it moves heat rather than making it, it uses much less electricity than a standard electric tank. Electricity in Denton comes from Denton Municipal Electric, the city-owned utility.

Most heat pump models also have backup electric elements for times of heavy demand, which use more power when they run.

Why a Denton garage can suit a heat pump

Most Denton water heaters live in the garage. For a heat pump, that is often a good spot. Garages in North Texas are warm for much of the year, and warm air is what a heat pump uses to heat water efficiently.

There is a bonus in summer. A heat pump cools and dries the air around it, which can make a hot garage a little more comfortable.

Winter is the trade-off. In a cold garage during a January freeze, a heat pump works less efficiently and leans more on its backup elements. Many models have settings that switch to electric-only mode in cold conditions. It still works. It just saves less during those weeks.

What to check before choosing a heat pump

Answer these first. They decide whether it fits.

  • Is there enough space and air around the unit? Many manufacturers specify a minimum room volume or a louvered door for closet installs.
  • Is there a drain nearby for the condensate the unit produces?
  • Is there a suitable electrical circuit, and room in the panel if a new one is needed?
  • Will the fan noise bother anyone? A garage is usually fine. A closet next to a bedroom may not be.
  • How fast does the household use hot water? Large back-to-back demand favors a bigger tank or a gas unit.
  • Is a permit needed? Water heater replacements in Denton typically require one, which your plumber handles.

When gas is still the better fit

Gas makes sense when the house already has a sized gas line and proper venting. It also suits a heater tucked into a small closet with little air. And it wins when the household needs fast recovery for many showers in a row.

It also makes sense if you want hot water during some power outages. A standing-pilot gas tank keeps heating without electricity, which matters to many North Texas homeowners after recent winter storms.

Whatever you choose, the venting, gas connection and relief valve discharge must meet the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) and its companion fuel gas code inside Denton. Our water heater installation page covers what a replacement involves.

Incentives and rebates: check before you buy

Federal tax credits for efficient water heaters changed in 2025, so do not rely on older articles that promise a specific credit. Check current IRS guidance or ask a tax professional before counting on one.

Utilities sometimes offer rebates too. Ask Denton Municipal Electric whether any water heater program is running, and ask Atmos Energy about gas appliance offers. Programs come and go, so confirm terms before you buy.

Rebates should not drive the decision on their own. A heater that does not suit the space or the household will cost more over its life than any rebate saves.

Maintenance differences

Both types benefit from a yearly flush to remove sediment, which matters on Denton's lake-sourced water. Both have an anode rod that should be checked every few years.

A heat pump adds two simple tasks: cleaning the air filter on the unit, often every few months, and keeping the condensate drain clear. A clogged filter cuts efficiency, and a blocked drain can leave water on the floor.

If your current heater is failing and you are not sure whether repair or replacement makes sense, our repair-or-replace guide for water heaters has a decision table by age. Homes in Corinth built in the 1990s and 2000s are often on their second heater, which is a good time to weigh the options.
