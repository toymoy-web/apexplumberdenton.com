Plumbing Guides for Denton, TX Homeowners

Practical guides built around the conditions Denton homes actually face: expansive clay, lake-sourced water, older pipe near downtown and a patchwork of water providers in the surrounding towns.

What these guides cover

Every guide here is written for homes in Denton and the nearby towns we serve. Each one starts from a real local condition. That might be the expansive clay that moves slab foundations, or water drawn from Lewisville Lake and Ray Roberts Lake.

Others cover the City of Denton's 2024 Stage 2 watering limits, backflow testing rules, and the wells and septic systems still common on the rural edges of the county. Where a guide mentions code, it refers to the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) the City adopted in 2022.

The goal is simple. Help you understand what is happening before anyone quotes a fix, and help you decide what you can check yourself and when to call. Each guide links to the matching service page if you need hands-on help.

Topics include slab leaks and foundation watering, reading your water meter, water heater life on mineral-heavy water, tankless descaling, and softeners versus filters.

Others cover galvanized pipe in older homes near the Square, landlord and tenant repair duties near UNT and TWU, tree roots in sewer lines, freeze preparation and sewer backups after heavy rain. The rest deal with heat pump water heaters, water heater permits, backflow testing and septic versus city sewer.

None of these guides replace an inspection of your own system. Plumbing varies from house to house, and a licensed plumber should look before any repair. When you are ready, the full list of plumbing services is one click away.
