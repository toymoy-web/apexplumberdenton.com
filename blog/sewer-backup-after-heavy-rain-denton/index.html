Sewage Backing Up After Heavy Rain in Denton: What to Do First

By the Denton Apex Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026.

A North Texas downpour can drop a lot of rain in a short time. Sometimes the first sign of trouble is not a flooded street but a sewer backup: water rising in a shower drain or a floor drain. This guide covers what to do in the first hour, how to tell whose problem it is and how to prevent the next one.

What to do in the first hour

Sewage carries bacteria. Safety and damage control come before anything else.

  • Stop using water everywhere in the house: no flushing, laundry, dishwasher or showers. Every gallon has to go somewhere, and the lowest drain is where it goes.
  • Keep children and pets out of the area. Wear gloves and boots if you must walk through it.
  • If water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay clear and switch off power only if you can reach the panel safely from a dry spot.
  • Photograph and video everything, including the time, before any cleanup.
  • Check with a neighbor or two. Whether they are backed up too tells you where the problem is.
  • Call the right party: the city if the main is the issue, a plumber if it is your line.

Why heavy rain causes sewer backups

Sanitary sewers are designed to carry wastewater from homes, not rainwater. But during heavy storms, rainwater and groundwater find their way in through cracked pipes, loose manhole covers, illegal downspout connections and defective cleanout caps. Engineers call this inflow and infiltration.

When enough extra water enters the system, the main in the street can run full. Wastewater then has nowhere to go, and it backs up the laterals into the lowest drains in houses connected to that main.

Your own lateral can also be the cause. A partly blocked line, from roots, grease or a sag, may drain fine on a dry day but back up when heavy rain raises the water table and more water finds its way into the pipe.

Cleanup: when to do it yourself and when not to

Sewage is classed as Category 3 water under the IICRC S500 restoration standard, the most contaminated category. Porous materials it soaks, such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, usually have to be removed rather than dried.

A small backup contained to a tile floor can sometimes be cleaned by the homeowner with gloves, boots, disinfectant and good ventilation. Anything that reached carpet, walls or a large area is usually a job for a restoration crew, both for safety and for a proper insurance record.

Restoration is a separate service from plumbing, and the drying and cleanup crew has its own line. The plumber's job is to clear the line and find out why it backed up.

Clearing the line and finding the cause

Once the city main is ruled out, the plumber clears your lateral, usually from the outdoor cleanout. A cable machine opens the line. Heavy grease or root mats may call for hydro jetting.

The more important step comes next. A sewer camera shows whether the line is cracked, separated, full of roots or sagging. A line that only backs up in storms often has a crack or open joint that lets groundwater in. Our drain cleaning page covers the clearing, and sewer line repair covers the fixes the camera may point to.

Ask for the camera video. It is useful for your insurer and for comparing repair quotes.

How to prevent the next backup

Some of these are simple. One is a real plumbing upgrade.

  • Keep the cleanout cap on and intact. A broken or missing cap lets rain and debris straight into your lateral.
  • Disconnect any downspout or yard drain tied into the sewer. Rainwater does not belong there.
  • Fix known cracks and root entry points found on camera.
  • Keep grease out of the kitchen drain, since a narrowed line backs up sooner in a storm.
  • Ask about a backwater valve. It closes automatically when sewage tries to flow back toward the house. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for backwater protection where fixtures sit below the next upstream sewer manhole, and many homes add one voluntarily.

Insurance: check before the next storm

Many standard homeowner policies exclude water that backs up through sewers or drains unless a specific sewer backup endorsement was added. It is usually an inexpensive add-on.

Read your policy now, not after a storm. If the endorsement is missing, ask your agent about the cost and the coverage limit.

Newer subdivisions are not immune. Homes around Aubrey and other fast-growing towns connect to newer mains, but construction debris, settling and storm inflow can still send water back up a lateral.

Denton note: who to call for a main line backup

The City of Denton's utility FAQ asks residents to report backups or overflows coming from the sewer main to the Wastewater Collections department. The lateral from your house to the main is the homeowner's responsibility.

If a plumber clears your lateral and finds the blockage is past it, the city asks you to call Utilities Dispatch. Homes in Lake Dallas and other nearby towns should call their own city's sewer department.
