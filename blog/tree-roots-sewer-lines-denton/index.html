Roots, laterals and older neighborhoods

Tree Roots in Sewer Lines: Post Oaks, Pecans and Older Laterals

By the Denton Apex Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026.

Mature shade trees are one of the best things about Denton's older neighborhoods. They are also one of the most common reasons a sewer line clogs every spring. This guide explains why roots get into sewer lines, how to spot it early and how the fixes compare.

Why roots find sewer lines

Denton sits in the Cross Timbers, where post oaks and other native hardwoods grow alongside planted pecans, cedar elms and other shade trees. Their roots spread wide and search constantly for water and nutrients.

A sewer lateral carries warm, nutrient-rich water. If a joint is loose or a pipe is cracked, a little vapor and moisture escape into the surrounding soil. Fine roots follow it to the source and slip through the gap.

Once inside, a root grows into a mat of hair-like fibers that catches paper and waste. The mat thickens every season until the line slows and backs up.

Why older Denton lines are most at risk

Older laterals were often made of clay tile or cast iron in short sections. Clay tile joints were not tightly sealed, and cast iron corrodes and cracks over time. Both give roots easy entry.

Denton's expansive clay makes it worse. As the soil swells and shrinks, it pulls on the joints, opening gaps a little more each year. Dry summers, when trees search hardest for water, tend to be followed by a wave of root clogs.

Modern PVC laterals with glued joints resist roots far better, which is why a single repair at the worst joint can buy many years.

Early signs of root intrusion

Roots rarely cause a sudden blockage. They build up.

  • Slow drains throughout the house, not just one fixture.
  • Gurgling toilets, especially when the washer drains.
  • Backups that return every few months or every spring.
  • A lush, green strip of lawn over the line's path.
  • A sewage odor near the cleanout.

What to do when the line backs up

Safety and damage control come first.

  • Stop using water everywhere in the house.
  • Keep children and pets away from any sewage.
  • Do not pour chemical drain openers into a main line. They rarely reach root clogs and make the line hazardous to open.
  • If neighbors on the same street are also backed up, call the City of Denton wastewater line, since the city main may be blocked.
  • If only your house is affected, call a plumber to clear the line and run a camera.

Clearing the line, then deciding

The first visit usually clears the blockage and restores flow. The second step matters more: a camera inspection. It shows where roots enter, how many joints are affected and whether the pipe is cracked, separated or sagging.

If the pipe is sound with fine roots, hydro jetting scours it clean. If there is one bad joint, a spot repair ends the problem at that spot. If several joints are failing, lining or replacement makes more sense. Those options are compared on our page about repairing a damaged lateral.

New sewer pipe is laid to the minimum slopes in the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), such as 1/8 inch of fall per foot for 4-inch pipe, which helps it flow freely and resist future buildup.

What a sewer camera report shows

A camera inspection records the inside of the lateral from the cleanout to the city main. The plumber should mark the distance to each problem, so you know exactly where it is.

Look for four things on the footage. Root intrusion appears as hair-like fibers at joints. Offset joints show as a step where one section no longer lines up with the next. Cracks show as dark lines, sometimes with soil visible. Bellies show as water that stays pooled in a low section.

Ask for a copy of the video. It is useful for comparing repair quotes, for planning future maintenance and for showing a buyer the line's condition if you sell.

The camera also shows the pipe material, which helps predict how the rest of the line will age. Clay tile and cast iron sections are the most likely to fail next.

Trees and sewer lines can coexist

Know where your lateral runs before you plant. It usually leaves the house near a bathroom or kitchen and heads toward the street, often passing the outdoor cleanout. A plumber can locate it with a transmitter on the camera.

You do not have to cut down a healthy tree to protect a sewer line. Fixing the entry point removes what attracts the roots. When planting new trees, keep water-seeking species well away from the known path of the lateral.

Acreage properties have longer laterals and often septic lines, which roots can invade too. Homes around Argyle with mature trees near drain fields should watch for the same warning signs.
