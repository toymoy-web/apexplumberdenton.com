Getting Denton Pipes Ready for a North Texas Hard Freeze

By the Denton Apex Plumbing team. Published September 24, 2026.

Denton does not freeze often, which is exactly why a hard freeze does so much damage. Homes built for mild winters put water lines in attics, exterior walls and garages, which is where frozen pipes start. The February 2021 winter storm showed what happens when those lines freeze across a whole region. This checklist is about the hours before the next one.

Why North Texas homes are vulnerable

In colder climates, builders keep water lines inside heated space and bury service lines deep. In North Texas, mild winters led to different habits. Supply lines often run through attics, along exterior walls and across unheated garages.

That works nearly every winter. During a long hard freeze, especially one with power outages, those exposed lines lose heat fast. Water in the pipe freezes and expands. The split usually happens somewhere else along the line, where pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet.

The February 2021 winter storm brought days of freezing temperatures and widespread power outages across Texas. Burst pipes were one of the most common kinds of damage afterward, because the heat that normally protects indoor lines was off.

What to do before a hard freeze

Start when the forecast calls for several hours below freezing.

  • Disconnect every garden hose from outdoor faucets and fit insulated covers over the spigots.
  • Shut off and drain the irrigation system, and insulate or drain an above-ground backflow assembly.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm room air reaches the pipes.
  • Let a faucet on an exterior wall drip slowly, both hot and cold, to keep water moving.
  • Keep the garage door closed if water lines or the water heater are in the garage.
  • Keep the thermostat steady, day and night, rather than setting it back.
  • Find the main shutoff and make sure it turns. Put a flashlight near it.
  • If you will be away, leave the heat on and consider shutting off the main and draining the lines.

If you lose power during a freeze

A power outage changes the plan. Without heat, indoor lines are at risk too, and built-in freeze protection on tankless units stops working.

If the outage looks long and temperatures are well below freezing, shutting off the main and draining the lines is the safest option. Open the lowest faucet in the house and an outdoor spigot to drain what you can. Drain a tankless unit according to its manual.

Never use an open flame to thaw pipes, and never run a gas oven or generator indoors for heat. Carbon monoxide is a bigger danger than a frozen pipe.

What to do if a pipe bursts

Often the leak only shows when the ice thaws. Act fast.

  • Close the main shutoff immediately.
  • Switch off power to wet areas if you can reach the breaker panel safely.
  • Turn off the water heater, or set gas to pilot, if hot lines are involved.
  • Open faucets to drain the remaining water.
  • Photograph the damage and the break for insurance.
  • Move belongings out of the water and call for help.

Our burst pipe repair service runs 24/7. If water has soaked walls or ceilings, drying is a separate job for the restoration team, which has its own line.

After the thaw: checking for hidden damage

Some breaks only leak once the ice melts, and some leak inside walls where you will not see them right away.

  • Run the meter test once the freeze ends: all fixtures off, watch the leak indicator.
  • Check the attic with a flashlight for wet insulation or drips.
  • Look at ceilings and walls below attic lines and along exterior walls for new stains.
  • Turn on each outdoor faucet and watch inside the wall behind it for leaks.
  • Check the irrigation system zone by zone before running it on a schedule.
  • Watch the next water bill for an unexplained jump.

A leak found in the first days after a freeze is far cheaper to fix than one found weeks later behind drywall.

Longer-term fixes

Take notes after every freeze: which faucet slowed first, which line burst, which room got cold. That record tells a plumber exactly where to add insulation or reroute a line before next winter.

If a line froze once, it will likely freeze again. Insulating attic and wall lines, adding a frost-free sillcock in place of a standard outdoor faucet, and adding a shutoff valve for outdoor lines all reduce the risk permanently.

Rural properties need extra attention. Lines to barns, shops and stock tanks sit in unheated space and often run long distances. Around Krum and other rural parts of the county, a separate shutoff for outbuilding lines lets you drain them before a freeze without shutting off the house.

Repairs and reroutes inside Denton follow the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which covers protecting water piping from freezing where it runs through unheated space.

Denton note: find your valves before the freeze

The City of Denton's lead service line FAQ tells owners where to look. The water meter box sits near the curb or sidewalk, and the outdoor spigot and shutoff valve are usually on an exterior wall. Find and test both before cold weather.

If a frozen line splits, the city's FAQ says a leak between the street and the meter box is the city's to fix. A leak between the meter box and the house is the owner's. Owners in Sanger should check with their own provider.
