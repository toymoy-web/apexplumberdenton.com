About Denton Apex Plumbing in Denton, TX

Denton Apex Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing line for Denton, TX and seven nearby towns. This page explains how calls are handled, who does the work, what we promise and what we do not.

Call (940) 229-7314 All plumbing services

What happens when you call

When you call, you are connected with a dispatcher who takes the details: what you see, where it is and how urgent it is. The job then goes to an independent, Texas-licensed plumbing professional who serves your part of Denton County.

We say that plainly because it matters. Calls may be answered by a service partner rather than by us directly, and the plumber who arrives sets the final price, schedule and warranty for the work they perform. That is also stated in the footer of every page.

Behind the scenes, the dispatcher also flags jobs that need something unusual: a gas smell, a home on a private well, a septic system, or a rental where the owner must approve the work. Getting those details right on the first call saves a second trip.

What stays the same is the process. The cause is found first, the price is explained before repairs begin, and non-emergency estimates are free.

What we commit to

These are the things we can honestly stand behind.

  • A line that is answered 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays.
  • Licensed plumbers. Texas requires plumbers to hold a license from the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners, and you can check any license on the board's website before work starts.
  • Price before work. Emergency repairs are priced before they begin. Planned work such as replacements and repipes gets a free estimate.
  • Work to code. Inside Denton city limits, that means the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) the City adopted in 2022, with permits and inspections where the job requires them.
  • Straight answers about repair versus replacement, including when a repair is the cheaper, better choice.

What we do not claim

You will not find star ratings, review counts, award badges or years-in-business figures on this site. We have not published any we cannot verify, and we would rather show you nothing than show you something invented.

We also keep services separate where they really are separate. Water damage drying and cleanup is handled on its own restoration line, listed on the water damage restoration page. Septic tank pumping is done by licensed haulers, and deep well pump work by well drillers. We will tell you when a job belongs to another trade.

And we do not make health claims about water quality. For that, your water provider's annual report and a certified lab test are the right sources.

Why this site talks so much about Denton

Plumbing problems are local. Denton sits on expansive clay at the edge of the Blackland Prairie, so slab leaks and cracked sewer laterals are common. City water comes from Lewisville Lake and Ray Roberts Lake, and mineral buildup in water heaters and fixtures follows. The 2024 Stage 2 drought period, with limits on foundation watering, stressed buried lines across town.

Older homes near the Courthouse-on-the-Square carry galvanized pipe and cast iron drains. Rentals near UNT and TWU wear out disposals and toilets. Surrounding towns get water from a mix of city wells, lake water and utility districts. Each of those facts changes what a plumber should check first, which is why every page on this site is written around them.

The guides on each service page, such as slab leak repair and leak detection, are meant to help you understand the problem before anyone quotes a fix.
