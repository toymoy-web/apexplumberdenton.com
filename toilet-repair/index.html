Clogged, running or leaking toilet: what the symptom means

Toilet repair covers the parts in the tank, the seal at the floor and the bowl itself. Most problems point to a specific part.

A toilet that gurgles when other fixtures drain points to the main line, not the toilet.

Emergency toilet repair for overflows and base leaks

A toilet that overflows, keeps rising after a flush, or leaks from the base onto the floor is an emergency because the water soaks into the subfloor fast.

Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. Lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill if the valve will not close.

If water is coming up in other drains too, the problem is likely the main line. Call the 24/7 emergency line and say so, so the right equipment comes on the first visit.

Why running toilets matter on a Denton water bill

A running toilet is the single most common cause of a high water bill with no visible leak. A worn flapper can let water trickle into the bowl for weeks, and the only sign is the next City of Denton bill.

The dye test finds it. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing, and look in the bowl. Color means the flapper is leaking.

Mineral-heavy water shortens the life of tank parts here. Scale stiffens flappers and gums up fill valves. On older toilets, it also clogs the small rim holes that make a flush strong. If your bill is still high after the toilets pass, leak detection is the next step.

What a toilet visit includes

Most toilet jobs are same-day work. A typical visit covers:

  • Tank rebuild: flapper, fill valve, flush valve and supply line as needed.
  • Reset: pulling the toilet, inspecting the flange and setting a new wax or foam seal.
  • Flange repair: fixing a broken or corroded flange so the toilet sits firm.
  • Full replacement: removing the old toilet and installing a new one, including a new supply line and shutoff if needed.
  • Clog clearing: auger work on the bowl and trap.

Toilet installation: choosing a new toilet

New toilets sold in the US are built to use far less water per flush than older models, and many meet the EPA WaterSense standard of 1.28 gallons per flush or less. On an older 3.5-gallon toilet, the savings show up quickly.

The measurement that matters most is the rough-in: the distance from the finished wall to the center of the floor drain. Most homes have a 12-inch rough-in, but some older homes differ. Buying the wrong one means it will not fit.

Comfort-height bowls sit a couple of inches taller and are easier for many adults, including residents of 55-plus communities like Robson Ranch. Elongated bowls take more room than round ones in a small bathroom.

What toilet repair and installation cost depend on

These are the main variables.

  • The part. A flapper costs very little. A flange repair costs more.
  • Whether the toilet has to be pulled.
  • Floor condition around the flange, especially if a slow leak softened the subfloor.
  • New toilet model, whether supplied by you or by the plumber.

If a leaking wax ring damaged the floor, insurance may cover it only if the leak was sudden. Slow leaks are usually excluded.
