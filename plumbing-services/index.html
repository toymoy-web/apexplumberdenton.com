How to pick the right service

Many plumbing problems show up somewhere other than where they start. Low hot water pressure at every tap usually points to the water heater or the supply pipe, not the faucets. A toilet that gurgles when the washer drains points to the main line, not the toilet.

A high water bill with no visible water points to leak detection first, then to slab leak repair or water line repair depending on what the meter test shows. Repeated clogs point to hydro jetting or sewer line repair rather than another round of snaking.

Denton's conditions shape the list too. Expansive clay under much of the city stresses buried lines, lake-sourced water leaves scale in heaters and fixtures, and the city's backflow program brings a yearly test for most homes with irrigation. Those three facts explain why slab leaks, water heaters and backflow testing sit high on this page.

If you are still not sure, call and describe what you see. The dispatcher will point the call to the right plumber and the right service.

What to do before you call in an emergency

These steps apply to most plumbing emergencies, whatever the service.

  • Close the main shutoff if water is escaping. On many Denton slab homes it is in the garage.
  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call Atmos Energy from outside before anything else.
  • Switch off power to a leaking water heater, or set a gas unit to pilot.
  • Stop using water anywhere in the house if sewage is backing up.
  • Photograph the problem for insurance, then call the 24/7 line.
