What Denton soil, water and housing do to your plumbing

Four local conditions explain a large share of the calls in this part of Denton County. Each one changes what a plumber should check first.

Clay that moves under the slab

Denton sits where the dark clay of the Blackland Prairie meets the sandier Cross Timbers. That clay swells after a wet spring and shrinks in a dry August, and a slab foundation rides the movement.

Supply lines and drains cast into the slab take the strain. Watch for a new crack in floor tile, a warm spot underfoot or a meter that spins with every fixture off. Each one points toward a slab leak worth locating before the next rain widens it.

Lake water and scale

City of Denton water is drawn from Lewisville Lake and Ray Roberts Lake, the reservoir about 10 miles north of town. Published hardness figures for Denton disagree widely, from moderately hard to very hard.

That spread is the reason to test your own tap before buying treatment equipment. An inexpensive test kit settles it. Either way, sediment builds up in tank water heaters here, and a yearly flush costs far less than a new tank.

The 2024 watering limits

In August 2024, two damaged filters at the Lake Lewisville treatment plant pushed the city into Stage 2 of its Drought Contingency Plan. From August 20 to October 8, outdoor watering dropped to once a week, with limits on foundation watering.

A slab that dries out unevenly can shift, and shifting is what cracks buried pipe. If new cracks showed up in drywall or tile after that fall, have the lines under the house checked now rather than after a failure.

Older homes downtown, rentals near campus

Houses in the Oak-Hickory Historic District and other older blocks near downtown may still carry galvanized supply pipe or cast iron drains. Galvanized pipe rusts from the inside and slowly chokes water pressure, which is usually the point where whole-home repiping is worth pricing.

Near UNT and TWU, high rental turnover means disposals, toilets and kitchen drains get hard use. Landlords and property managers can use the same line, and the plumber can coordinate access with tenants.

Before the plumber gets there: a five-minute checklist

Most water damage in a plumbing emergency happens in the first half hour. These steps cut it down, and none of them need tools beyond a flashlight.

  • Find the house shutoff. On many Denton slab homes it sits in the garage or where the line enters the house. The city meter box near the street has a valve too, but it usually needs a meter key.
  • Cut energy to a leaking water heater. Flip the breaker for an electric unit, or turn a gas control knob to pilot or off.
  • After the main is closed, open an outdoor hose bib to drain the pressure left in the pipes.
  • Photograph the damage and note the time. Insurers and landlords ask for both.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the water path.

Cannot find the shutoff? Say so when you call, and the dispatcher will help you look for it.

What changes the price of a plumbing repair in Denton

No honest plumber can quote a leak they have not seen. These five factors decide most of the final number, so it helps to know them before the visit.

  • Time of the call. Nights, weekends and holidays usually cost more than a weekday appointment.
  • Access. A line under a slab or behind tile takes more labor to reach than one under a sink.
  • Pipe material and age. Galvanized steel, cast iron and polybutylene repairs often need adapters and extra parts.
  • Permits. Larger jobs such as water heater replacement or sewer work may need a City of Denton permit and inspection.
  • Equipment choice. A tank heater versus tankless, or a rebuild kit versus a new fixture, moves the total the most.

A note on insurance: homeowner policies often cover sudden water damage from something like a burst pipe. They usually do not pay to fix the pipe itself, and they rarely cover damage from a slow leak. Check your policy wording before assuming either way.
