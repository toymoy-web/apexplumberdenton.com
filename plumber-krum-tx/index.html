A small town with two kinds of plumbing

Krum's plumbing calls split cleanly by the age of the property. On the older rural places, the calls involve pumps, pressure tanks, septic tanks and buried runs out to barns and shops. In the newer subdivisions, they involve fixtures, water heaters and the odd crack under a foundation after its first few dry summers.

The city runs its own water utility from City Hall on W. McCart Street. Its fee schedule includes a North Texas Groundwater Conservation District charge on water use, and the city issues permits to drill new wells, repair well heads and plug old wells. That tells you groundwater is still part of life here.

Knowing which system your home is on is the first question the plumber will ask, so have it ready when you call.

Newer Krum subdivisions are almost all on city water and sewer, with PEX supply lines and PVC drains. Their problems look like those in any new Denton County home, which is why heater fixes and fixture calls from these streets resemble calls from Corinth or Aubrey.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak in Krum

The right first move depends on the water source: city service or a private well.

Close the main

In town, the shutoff is usually in the garage or at the meter box by the street. On a well property, switch off the well pump breaker to stop the flow.

Cut power to wet areas

Barns and shops often have outlets close to the floor. Switch off those circuits before anyone walks into standing water.

Clear the immediate area

Move feed, tools and stored goods off the floor in outbuildings. In the house, lift rugs and pull boxes away from the leak.

Before you switch from a well to city water

Connecting an older Krum home to city water is more than one new pipe. Plan for these steps.

  • Confirm the tap and meter process and fees with the City of Krum utility office.
  • Have the plumber check the house piping and the new service line route from the meter.
  • Decide what happens to the old well. The city has permit rules for plugging or keeping one.
  • Make sure the well and city systems are fully separated so well water cannot backflow into the city supply. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) prohibits any connection between a private well and a public water system without approved protection.
  • Recheck the water heater and any treatment equipment, since city water may need different settings.

The new buried line from the meter to the house is covered on our water line repair and replacement page.

Freezes, barns and outbuildings

Rural properties around Krum often have water lines running to barns, shops, stock tanks and outdoor spigots. Those lines sit in unheated spaces, and they are the first to freeze in a North Texas cold snap.

A split line in a barn can run for days before anyone notices, especially on a well where there is no meter to watch. Insulating exposed lines, adding a shutoff for outbuildings and draining them before a hard freeze prevent most of it.

When a line does burst, shut off water to that branch or the whole property, then call. The steps are covered in more detail under split and frozen pipe repairs.

Krum's holiday season, North Pole Days, usually falls right when the first hard freezes arrive. It is a good reminder to wrap outdoor lines before the cold does it for you.
