When repair is no longer enough

Sewer Line Replacement in Denton, TX

Some sewer lines are past patching. A collapsed section, a lateral that sags for most of its length or decades-old pipe failing in several places all point to replacement. Sewer line replacement in Denton, TX swaps the whole private lateral, from the house to the city connection.

Talk through a replacement All plumbing services

Replacement versus repair

Sewer line replacement means installing a new lateral for the full run between the house and the city main, rather than fixing one section. It is a separate job from a repair, and the difference matters for your budget.

Repair makes sense when the camera shows one or two defects in otherwise sound pipe. Replacement makes sense when the defects are many, the pipe has lost its shape, or the line has sagged badly enough that waste pools along much of its length.

A useful rule: if you are facing a third repair on the same line, price a replacement alongside it. For one-off defects, smaller sewer repairs are often enough.

Open trench or trenchless?

There are two main ways to replace a lateral. The ground above the line usually decides.

Sags and bellies usually require open trench work, since bursting follows the old pipe's path.

Why Denton lines sag and fail

Expansive Blackland Prairie clay is the main culprit across much of Denton County. It lifts and drops a buried pipe with each wet and dry season. Over years, a lateral that was laid with a steady slope can develop low spots where waste settles and hardens.

Droughts make it worse. When the city limited foundation watering during the Stage 2 period from August 20 to October 8, 2024, soil around many homes dried and shrank. Uneven shrinkage is exactly the kind of movement that pulls sewer joints apart.

New pipe does not stop the soil from moving, but modern PVC laid on proper bedding with the correct slope handles it far better than clay tile or cast iron. If the old line needs clearing before the camera survey, a hydro jet cleaning gives a cleaner view than cabling.

How a replacement job runs

Most full replacements follow this order.

  • Camera inspection and locating to map the line, its depth and its connection point.
  • Permit application with the City of Denton where required.
  • Utility locates, so gas, electric and water lines are marked before digging.
  • Excavation or pit digging, then removal or bursting of the old pipe.
  • New pipe laid with correct slope, a cleanout added if needed, and an inspection before backfill.
  • Backfill and surface restoration, then a final camera run to confirm the new line.

Adding a cleanout while the line is open

Many older homes have no outdoor cleanout, or only one inside the house. That makes every future clog harder and more expensive to reach, since the plumber has to pull a toilet or go through a roof vent.

A replacement is the easiest time to add a two-way cleanout near the foundation or at the property line. It costs little while the trench is open and gives direct access to the new line for camera checks and cleaning later.

What sewer line replacement costs depend on

Replacement is a larger project. These variables set the price.

  • Length and depth of the line.
  • Method. Trenchless bursting can cost less than trenching under a driveway, but not every line qualifies.
  • What sits above the line, such as concrete, a patio or mature trees.
  • Permit and inspection requirements.
  • Restoration of lawn, concrete or flooring afterward.

Planned replacement estimates are free. Ask for the camera footage so you can see the reasons for the recommendation.
