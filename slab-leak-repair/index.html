Leaks under the foundation

Emergency Slab Leak Detection & Repair in Denton, TX

Concrete foundations on Denton clay carry copper or PEX lines locked inside them, and those lines crack when the slab flexes. Slab leak detection and repair in Denton, TX starts by isolating hot from cold, traces the run through the concrete, then lays out the honest options: a spot repair, a reroute or a repipe. Warm floor, hissing wall or a bill that keeps climbing?

Nobody sees a slab leak directly. People notice what it does. These are the signs Denton homeowners describe most often.

  • A warm or hot spot on a tile or concrete floor. That usually means a hot-water line is leaking underneath.
  • The sound of running water with every fixture off. It is often loudest near a wall or in a closet.
  • A meter dial that never rests. The utility gauge spins even with sprinklers and faucets shut.
  • New cracks, loose baseboards or soggy carpet. Nothing overhead explains them.
  • A water heater that runs constantly. It keeps reheating water that is leaking away.

Any one of these is worth a meter test. Two or more together are worth a call. If water is surfacing through the floor, the line is open 24/7.

Emergency slab leak signs that need a call today

Warm spots on the floor, water coming up through flooring, or a meter that keeps moving with everything off can all mean a pressurized line is leaking under the slab. Treat it as an emergency.

Close the main shutoff to stop the loss. If hot water is involved, turn off the water heater as well so it does not run empty.

Call the 24/7 line. The emergency visit confirms the leak, keeps the water off safely and explains the repair choices, spot repair or a reroute, with a price before work starts.

Why slab leaks are common in Denton

A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs beneath or inside a home's concrete foundation. In this part of Texas, the ground itself is a big part of the cause.

Denton sits where the dark, expansive clay of the Blackland Prairie meets the Cross Timbers. That clay swells after heavy rain and shrinks during dry stretches. The slab moves with it, and pipes locked in concrete cannot move freely. Over years, that stress opens pinholes and cracks fittings.

Dry summers make it worse. In 2024 the city spent August 20 to October 8 under Stage 2 drought rules after filter damage at the Lake Lewisville treatment plant, with limits on foundation watering. Uneven drying around a slab is exactly the kind of movement that stresses buried lines. If floors or drywall cracked that fall, it is worth a meter test now.

Pipe material adds to it. Many older homes have copper under the slab, which can pit over decades. Newer homes often use PEX, which handles movement better but can still fail at fittings.

How a slab leak gets pinpointed

Confirming the leak comes first. The hot and cold sides are isolated and pressure-tested separately with a gauge, which shows which system is losing water.

Next, the line is traced. An electronic locator follows the pipe's path through the slab, since the actual route rarely matches what anyone remembers.

Then the spot is found. Acoustic ground microphones pick up the hiss of escaping water through concrete. On hot-side leaks, a thermal camera shows the warm bloom on the floor surface. The two methods together usually narrow the leak to a small area.

This is the same staged method used for any hidden leak detection job. The difference is that under a slab, the stakes of guessing wrong are a lot higher.

Repair options, side by side

Once the leak is pinpointed, there are three realistic paths. The right one depends on the pipe's age and how many leaks the house has had.

Reroutes and repipes in Denton are done to the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), usually with PEX tubing made to the ASTM F876 standard. When the whole system is at fault, whole-home repiping is often the more economical long-term choice.

Slab leak hot spots in Denton

The Blackland Prairie clay under much of south and east Denton moves the most, and slab homes from Southridge out to the subdivisions east of Loop 288 see the bulk of slab leak calls.

Homes on the sandier Cross Timbers soils to the west move less, so a warm floor there is still worth checking but tends to be rarer.

What slab leak repair costs depend on

These are the factors that decide the final number.

  • How quickly the leak is pinpointed. A clean hot-side leak is faster to find than a small cold-side pinhole.
  • Floor covering. Lifting and replacing tile or hardwood above the leak adds cost.
  • Repair path. Spot repair, reroute and repipe differ widely in labor and material.
  • Foundation condition. If the slab itself has moved, a foundation specialist may need to be involved.
  • Permits. Larger reroutes and repipes may need a City of Denton plumbing permit and inspection.

Many homeowner policies cover the cost of breaking and restoring the floor to reach a sudden leak, even when they will not pay for the pipe repair. Ask your insurer before work begins.
