Hydro Jetting in Denton, TX

If a drain was snaked last month and it is already slow again, the cable probably punched a hole through the clog without clearing it. Hydro jetting in Denton, TX scours the full inside of the pipe with high-pressure water, and a camera check first makes sure the line can take it.

Ask about hydro jetting All plumbing services

Snaked it and it clogged again?

That is the most common reason people end up asking about jetting. A cable machine bores a path through a blockage, which gets water moving, but grease, scale and root fibers stay stuck to the pipe walls. Within weeks, new debris catches on what was left.

Hydro jetting is a drain cleaning method that uses water at very high pressure, through a hose with a rear-facing nozzle, to scour the pipe wall clean. The nozzle pulls itself up the line while the jets cut through grease and fine roots and flush the debris downstream.

Other signs point the same way. A cable got stuck partway out. A restaurant kitchen near the Square, or a rental kitchen near UNT, clogs on a schedule. A sewer lateral under Cross Timbers oaks fills with roots every spring.

When jetting is the right tool

Jetting shines on some problems and is the wrong call on others.

  • Grease-packed kitchen lines, common in older homes and busy rental kitchens.
  • Fine root intrusion in a line that is otherwise sound.
  • Sludge and scale in cast iron lines that have narrowed over decades.
  • Commercial drains such as restaurant lines and floor drains that need scheduled cleaning.
  • Not for broken pipe. A cracked, separated or collapsed line can be made worse. That is a repair job, not a cleaning job.

Cable cleaning versus hydro jetting

Both have a place. The difference is what they leave behind.

For one-off clogs in a sink or tub, standard drain cleaning is usually enough.

Why the camera goes in first

Denton's clay soil moves sewer lines over time. Joints separate, sections sag and older clay or cast iron pipe cracks. Jetting a line in that condition can wash out soil around the break.

That is why a responsible jetting job starts with a sewer camera. The plumber runs a waterproof camera through the cleanout, checks the pipe material and condition, and notes where roots enter. If the pipe is sound, jetting goes ahead. If it is broken, the footage shows exactly where the damaged lateral needs repair.

The camera run afterward is just as useful. It confirms the line is clean and gives you a record of the pipe's condition.

After the jetting: keeping the line clear

A clean line stays clean longer with a few habits. Grease belongs in the trash, not the sink. Wipes belong in the trash even when the package says flushable.

For roots, the entry point matters. If the camera showed roots coming in at one joint, a spot repair or liner at that joint ends the regrowth, which saves repeated jetting.

What affects hydro jetting cost

Jetting costs more than cabling. These are the variables.

  • Line length and diameter. A 4-inch main line takes more time than a 2-inch kitchen branch.
  • Cleanout access. A usable outdoor cleanout keeps the job simple.
  • Severity. Heavy root mats or decades of grease take longer passes.
  • Camera inspection before and after, which is strongly recommended.
  • Commercial scheduling, such as after-hours work in a restaurant.

Ask for the camera footage. It shows what you paid for and helps plan future maintenance.

Hydro jetting questions

It is safe for sound pipe of most materials, including older cast iron in fair condition. It is not safe for cracked or collapsed sections, which is why a camera check comes first.

Homes often only need it once, then only if problems return. Restaurants and busy commercial kitchens may need it on a schedule, depending on grease load.
