Water Filtration Installation in Denton, TX

Tap water that tastes like a pool, smells faintly of chlorine or leaves grit in the aerator. Water filtration installation in Denton, TX starts with what is actually in your water. The fix might be one under-sink unit or a whole-house carbon filter.

Ask about filtration All plumbing services

Taste, smell or just not sure what is in it?

Most people call about filtration because of how the water tastes or smells, not because of a test result. Chlorine-type taste, a musty note in late summer or sediment in the aerator are common reasons.

Water filtration installation means putting a filter system on your supply, either at the point where water enters the house or at a single tap. Denton treats lake water from Lewisville Lake and Ray Roberts Lake to federal drinking water standards, so filtration here is usually about taste, odor and preference rather than safety.

The City of Denton publishes an annual water quality report. Reading it, or testing your own tap, tells you which filter type actually addresses your concern.

Home water filtration systems: which filter does what

Each type targets different things. None does everything.

Check your utility's report for which disinfectant it uses. It decides the carbon type.
SystemRemoves or reducesWhere it goes
Sediment filter.Sand, rust flakes and grit.Whole house, before other filters.
Whole-house carbon.Chlorine taste and odor, some organics.At the main line, often in the garage.
Catalytic carbon.Chloramine, which standard carbon handles poorly.At the main line.
Reverse osmosis.Dissolved solids and many contaminants.Under the kitchen sink, one tap.
Water softener.Hardness minerals only.At the main line.

If scale on fixtures is the real complaint, a water softener is the right tool, not a filter.

Standards worth asking about

Filters sold for drinking water are commonly certified to NSF/ANSI standards. NSF/ANSI 42 covers taste and smell, such as chlorine. NSF/ANSI 58 covers reverse osmosis. Asking which standard a product is certified to tells you what it has been tested to do.

Installation inside Denton city limits follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC). Reverse osmosis drain lines need a proper air gap so waste water cannot be drawn back into the unit.

A whole-house system also reduces pressure slightly as cartridges load up. If pressure drops noticeably, the cartridge is due, or the system was undersized. Pressure problems elsewhere may point to aging supply pipe instead.

What to do before choosing a filter

A good install follows a short process.

  • Name the problem: taste, smell, grit or one thing you tested for.
  • Review the city water report or test the tap.
  • Choose whole-house, point-of-use or both, and size for household flow.
  • Install with a bypass valve and shutoffs so cartridges can be changed without draining the house.
  • Set a cartridge replacement schedule, since spent filters stop working and can restrict flow.

Filtration requests around Denton

Taste and odor questions come from all over the city, but under-sink reverse osmosis is especially popular in newer kitchens around Rayzor Ranch and the southwest side.

Rural properties outside city service on the north and west edges of the county may be on private wells, which need their own lab test before any equipment.

Private wells on the edges of the county

Homes outside city water service, including some rural properties around Krum, Ponder and Aubrey, draw from private wells. Public water reports do not apply to them, so filtration has to start with a lab test of the well water itself.

Well water can carry iron, sulfur odor, sediment or bacteria that city water does not. Each needs its own treatment step, and the order of equipment matters.

The Texas A&M AgriLife Extension runs well water screening programs in many counties. A screening is a low-cost first step before buying equipment.

Once the results are back, the plumber can match equipment to the actual problem, whether that is a sediment filter, an iron filter or a disinfection step.

What filtration costs depend on

These factors decide the price.

  • System type and capacity.
  • Whole-house versus a single tap.
  • New piping loop and bypass at the main line.
  • Cartridge replacement schedule and cost over time.

Ask about the yearly cartridge cost before choosing a system. It adds up.
