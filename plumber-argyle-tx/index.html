Argyle's water supply

Much of Argyle is served by the Argyle Water Supply Corporation. Its filings with the Public Utility Commission of Texas list storage tanks with wells on Taylor Road, FM 407 West and Fincher Road, and two of those sites are also delivery points for water from the Upper Trinity Regional Water District.

In practice, that means the supply blends groundwater with purchased lake water. As in Sanger and Aubrey, the mineral content can vary, so test at your own tap before buying treatment equipment.

Long service lines on acreage also lose pressure over distance, especially when the irrigation runs at the same time as showers. A pressure test at a hose bib near the house tells you what the fixtures are actually getting.

Warning signs of septic trouble

Many older Argyle properties use septic systems. Catching trouble early costs far less than a failed drain field.

  • Slow drains throughout the house, not just one fixture.
  • Gurgling in toilets or tubs after heavy water use.
  • Sewage odor near the tank or over the field.
  • Unusually green, soggy grass over the drain field in dry weather.
  • Backups after heavy rain, when a saturated field cannot absorb more.

A septic hauler pumps the tank. A plumber clears the house drains and the line to the tank, which our drain cleaning service covers. The drain piping inside the house is built to the International Plumbing Code (IPC), while the tank and field fall under the state's on-site sewage rules administered by the TCEQ.

Keep grease, wipes and heavy disposal use out of a septic system. Those are the usual reasons a tank needs pumping sooner than expected.

Irrigation, backflow and summer demand

Large lawns mean large irrigation systems. Texas requires irrigation systems on a public water supply to have an approved backflow prevention assembly, and utilities around Denton County require those assemblies to be tested. Your provider's notice will say when yours is due. The page on how the yearly backflow test works walks through each step.

Irrigation lines are also a common source of hidden water loss on acreage. A cracked lateral line or a valve that will not fully close can waste water for a whole season.
