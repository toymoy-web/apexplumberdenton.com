Where Sanger's water comes from, and why it matters

Sanger's water quality reports describe two sources. The city pumps groundwater from six wells in the Trinity Aquifer inside city limits. It also buys treated surface water from the Upper Trinity Regional Water District, drawn from Lewisville Lake.

A blended supply means the water at your tap is not always the same. Groundwater and lake water carry different mineral loads, and the mix can shift with demand and season. Homeowners sometimes notice more spotting on glassware or more scale on fixtures at certain times of year.

The blend also affects taste. Some residents notice a slight difference in how the water tastes or smells from season to season, which is normal for a mixed supply and not a sign of a problem with your pipes.

That is why a softener in Sanger should be sized from a test at your own faucet, ideally more than one test over a few months. Our water softener installation page explains how sizing works.

Older homes downtown, new streets on the edges

Sanger has an older core near its historic downtown and newer subdivisions spreading out from I-35. The two halves of town tend to have different plumbing problems.

Older homes are more likely to have galvanized supply pipe or cast iron drains. Galvanized pipe rusts inside and slowly cuts water pressure, while cast iron drains corrode and snag waste. A drain cleaning visit with a camera shows which you are dealing with.

Replacing old pipe in town usually needs a permit. Texas cities adopt either the International Plumbing Code (IPC) or the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and the City of Sanger permit office confirms which edition applies before work starts.

Newer homes usually have PEX supply lines and PVC drains. Their common problems are fixture and water heater issues, plus the occasional slab leak as the clay settles under a new foundation.
