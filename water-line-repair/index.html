Emergency Water Line Repair & Replacement in Denton, TX

A buried service line links the city meter to your house. It usually sits a foot or more down in clay. Water line repair in Denton, TX covers that run. It means locating the line, repairing a split, or replacing old galvanized or polybutylene pipe by trench or trenchless boring, with 24/7 response when it breaks open. Emergency water line repair & replacement calls are answered 24/7.

Wet yard, weak pressure or a meter that will not stop?

Few people think about this pipe until it fails. It sits underground, and its problems show up only indirectly, in the lawn, the pressure or the bill.

Water line repair and replacement covers that private line. It is separate from the plumbing inside the house, which is covered on our repiping page. In Denton, the utility maintains the meter and the line on the street side of it. The line from the meter to the house is usually the owner's.

The simplest test: close the house shutoff and watch the meter's leak indicator. If it still moves, water is leaving between the meter and the house.

Emergency water line repair when the yard floods

Water bubbling up in the yard, a sudden drop to no pressure, or a meter box full of water can mean the line from the meter to the house has broken. That is an emergency.

Close the house shutoff. If the break is between the meter and that valve, contact the water utility about the meter side and call our 24/7 line for the private line.

The emergency visit locates the break, repairs or replaces the damaged section and restores water once the line holds pressure.

Signs of a service line leak

Watch for these, especially after a hard freeze or a long dry spell.

  • A soggy strip of yard between the meter and the house in dry weather.
  • The meter moves with the house shutoff closed and the irrigation off.
  • Pressure dropped at every fixture at once.
  • Water pooling around the meter box. Call the city first if the leak is at the meter itself.
  • A sudden jump on the City of Denton water bill with no change in use.

Why service lines fail in Denton

Expansive clay pulls on buried lines every season. It swells after heavy rain and shrinks in dry spells, which stresses fittings and older pipe along the full length of the run.

Material matters too. Older homes may have galvanized steel lines that rust through, or polybutylene lines that grow brittle. Copper can corrode where it meets aggressive soil.

Replacement lines are installed and pressure-tested under the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) the City enforces. The same staged method for finding hidden leaks works here: an isolation test to confirm the yard line is losing water, a locator to trace its path, and acoustic listening to pinpoint the break before digging.

Repair or replace the line?

A single break in newer pipe can be repaired. An old, failing line is usually better replaced.

Replacement lines are commonly PEX or HDPE, laid below frost depth with proper bedding.

Service line issues by area

Long yard lines on larger lots toward the north and west edges of Denton, near Denton Enterprise Airport, are more exposed to ground movement and freeze damage.

In established neighborhoods like Denia and Southridge, older service lines in galvanized or early plastic are the usual culprits when a yard stays wet.

What happens to your yard

An open-trench repair digs along the section of line being fixed or replaced. Sod is cut and set aside where possible, and the trench is backfilled and compacted when the work is done.

Trenchless boring avoids most of that. Two small pits, one near the meter and one near the house, let new line be pulled underground beneath lawns, flower beds, sidewalks and driveways.

Utility locates happen before any digging. Gas, electric, cable and irrigation lines are marked so the crew can work around them.

What water line work costs depend on

These factors decide most of the price.

  • Length of the line from the meter to the house.
  • Repair versus full replacement.
  • What sits on top: lawn, landscaping, a driveway or a sidewalk.
  • Trenching versus trenchless boring.
  • Permits and utility locates before digging.

Many policies exclude service line wear unless a service line endorsement was added. Check your policy.
